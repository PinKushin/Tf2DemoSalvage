**The owner, 2026-09-10, after I said "good rule" to an idea and then agreed with him abandoning it
two messages later:**

> *"'good rule' then you immediately agree with me its not really worth it lol. I need to make sure
> you to let you know when im just brainstorming and thinking out loud, vs when i have made a real
> decision. again though, dont be afraid to argue with me over stuff, i need to know if something im
> thinking has unintended consequences i havent thought of."*

**Why it matters:** neither of my two responses was an opinion. Endorsing an idea and then endorsing
its withdrawal is agreement wearing judgement's clothes, and it is worth less than nothing to him —
it removes the second opinion he was relying on having. He proposed suffixing `B###` numbers per
agent so parallel sessions cannot collide; I called it a good rule without evaluating it, and when he
talked himself out of it I agreed again.

**How to apply:**

- **Evaluate before endorsing.** If a proposal has a consequence he has not mentioned, say it in the
  same message he proposes it — the moment to argue is before he has spent thought withdrawing it.
- **Say which part I disagree with, and keep the rest.** His reversal was right (suffixed numbers
  imply kinship between unrelated defects, which is worse than a plain collision) and his "not
  needed" was too broad (prevention is not worth it; DETECTION is nearly free, since
  `assert-decision-numbers.sh` already runs in the gate and could check `B###` too). Both halves
  belong in the answer.
- **He will not always mark which mode he is in**, so read it: a proposal with "i think" or "idk" or
  a question mark is thinking aloud, and is exactly where an argument is useful. A flat statement, or
  anything restating a standing decision, is settled.
- **Disagreement is the service.** He has asked for it repeatedly — see
  [[state-the-assumptions-the-owner-can-falsify]] and [[name-the-reading-you-picked]]. Deferring
  costs him the check he wanted.

This is the same failure as agreeing with a peer session's finding without reproducing it: an opinion
I did not form is not evidence, whoever it agrees with.
